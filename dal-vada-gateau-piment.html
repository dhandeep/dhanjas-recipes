Moong Dal Vada / Gâteau Piment

◆ Soak: 2–12 hrs (varies) ◆ Cook: ~30 min ◆ ~14 vadas + toddler
~10–12g protein per adult serving — from moong dal or split peas
🌙 Night Before — pick your version
  • Indian moong dal vada: soak yellow moong dal (split, skinned) (1 cup) 2–3 hours
  • Mauritian gâteau piment: soak yellow split peas (1 cup) 8–12 hours — they need much longer
  • Drain well before grinding
Ingredients
Vada base
  • Moong dal or split peas, soaked1 cup
  • Fresh ginger, grated1 tbsp
  • Hing¼ tsp
  • Cumin seeds1 tsp
  • Coriander seeds, crushed1 tsp
  • Turmeric¼ tsp
  • Saltto taste
Adult spicing (after toddler pull)
  • Green chili, chopped1–2
  • Curry leaves (Indian version)10 leaves
  • Fresh thyme (Mauritian version)1 tbsp
  • Fresh cilantro, chopped3 tbsp
Toddler portion & frying
  • Boiled potato, mashed (toddler)3 tbsp
  • Ghee, for her batter1 tsp
  • Ghee, for her patties1 tbsp
  • Neutral oil, for fryingas needed
Method
1 Grind and season the base
  • Drain the soaked dal or split peas (1 cup) well — split peas feel firmer than dal even after a long soak
  • Add to a blender with ginger (1 tbsp) and salt (a pinch)
  • Pulse to a coarse, thick paste — no added water; batter should hold its shape on a spoon. Split peas grind a bit coarser than moong dal — a few small flecks are normal and give gâteau piment its characteristic bite.
  • Too wet? Add a spoon of chickpea flour. Too dry? A few drops of water at a time.
  • Transfer to a mixing bowl; mix in hing (¼ tsp), cumin seeds (1 tsp), crushed coriander seeds (1 tsp) and turmeric (¼ tsp)
  • Mix in the remaining salt (to taste) — this is the shared base, no chili has gone in yet
👶 Toddler portion — pull now
  • Scoop out about ⅓ cup of the seasoned base into a separate bowl before any chili is added
  • Fold in mashed potato (3 tbsp) and ghee (1 tsp) to soften the texture
  • Set aside — this bowl stays out of all remaining adult steps
2 Spice and fry the adult batch
  • Into the remaining base, mix green chili (1–2), your herb — curry leaves (10 leaves) for the Indian version, or fresh thyme (1 tbsp) for Mauritian — and cilantro (3 tbsp)
  • Heat oil (as needed) in a kadai or heavy pot over medium-high heat
  • Wet hands, shape adult batter into small flat patties (~2 tbsp each) — for gâteau piment, shape slightly thicker in the center than a typical vada
  • Fry in batches until deep golden and firm — 3–4 minutes per side for moong dal, 4–5 minutes for split peas, which take a little longer to cook through
  • Drain on a rack or paper towel
3 Cook the toddler patties
  • Heat ghee (1 tbsp) in a small nonstick pan over medium-low heat
  • Shape the toddler batter into 3–4 small, thin patties (thinner than the adult ones, for a soft bite)
  • Pan-sear 2–3 minutes per side until set through and lightly golden — no raw batter should remain
  • Cool and cut into soft strips before serving to reduce choking risk
Notes These are culinary cousins — both trace back to Indian indentured-labor cooking traditions, but diverged: Indian moong dal vada uses skinned split mung bean and curry leaves; Mauritian gâteau piment traditionally uses yellow split peas and thyme (often chives too, swapped here for cilantro to keep it Jain). Toor dal isn't offered as a third option — it's too soft and crumbly to hold together fried this way. Lighter version: brush patties with oil and air-fry in the Ninja Crispi at 350°F for 8–10 minutes, flipping halfway — add 2–3 minutes for split peas, and check early since the Crispi runs hot; toddler patties stay pan-seared, their thin shape dries out in the Crispi. Leftover vadas keep 3 days refrigerated and re-crisp in a dry pan, or the Crispi at 330°F for 3–4 minutes.
