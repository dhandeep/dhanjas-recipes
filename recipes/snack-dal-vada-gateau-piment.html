Moong Dal Vada / Gâteau Piment

◆ Soak: 2–12 hrs (varies) ◆ Cook: ~30 min ◆ ~14 vadas + toddler
~10–12g protein per adult serving — from moong dal or split peas
🌙 Night Before — pick your version
  • Indian moong dal vada: soak 1 cup yellow moong dal (split, skinned) 2–3 hours
  • Mauritian gâteau piment: soak 1 cup yellow split peas 8–12 hours — they need much longer
  • Drain well before grinding
Ingredients
Vada base
  • Moong dal or split peas, soaked1 cup
  • Fresh ginger, grated1 tbsp
  • Hing¼ tsp
  • Cumin seeds1 tsp
  • Coriander seeds, crushed1 tsp
  • Turmeric¼ tsp
  • Saltto taste
Adult spicing (after toddler pull)
  • Green chili, chopped1–2
  • Curry leaves (or thyme)10 leaves
  • Fresh cilantro, chopped3 tbsp
Toddler portion
  • Boiled potato, mashed3 tbsp
  • Ghee1 tsp
Frying
  • Neutral oil, for fryingas needed
  • Ghee, for toddler patties1 tbsp
Method
1 Grind the base
  • Drain the soaked dal or split peas (1 cup) well — split peas feel firmer than dal even after a long soak
  • Add to a blender with ginger (1 tbsp) and a pinch of the salt
  • Pulse to a coarse, thick paste — no added water; batter should hold its shape on a spoon. Split peas grind a bit coarser than moong dal — a few small flecks are normal and give gâteau piment its characteristic bite.
  • Transfer to a mixing bowl
2 Season the base
  • Mix in hing (¼ tsp), cumin seeds (1 tsp), crushed coriander seeds (1 tsp), and turmeric (¼ tsp)
  • Mix in remaining salt to taste
  • This is the shared base — no chili has gone in yet
👶 Toddler portion — pull now
  • Scoop out about ⅓ cup of the seasoned base into a separate bowl before any chili is added
  • Fold in mashed potato (3 tbsp) and ghee (1 tsp) to soften the texture
  • Set aside — this bowl stays out of all remaining adult steps
3 Spice the adult batter
  • Into the remaining base, mix in green chili (1–2), and your chosen herb — curry leaves (10 leaves) for the Indian version, or fresh thyme (1 tbsp) for Mauritian — plus cilantro (3 tbsp)
  • Mix evenly — this is now the adult batter only
4 Shape and fry the adult batch
  • Heat oil (as needed) in a kadai or heavy pot over medium-high heat
  • Wet hands, shape adult batter into small flat patties (~2 tbsp each) — for gâteau piment, shape slightly thicker in the center than a typical vada
  • Fry in batches until deep golden and firm — 3–4 minutes per side for moong dal, 4–5 minutes for split peas, which take a little longer to cook through
  • Drain on a rack or paper towel
5 Cook the toddler patties
  • Heat ghee (1 tbsp) in a small nonstick pan over medium-low heat
  • Shape the toddler batter into 3–4 small, thin patties (thinner than the adult ones, for a soft bite)
  • Pan-sear 2–3 minutes per side until set through and lightly golden — no raw batter should remain
  • Cool and cut into soft strips before serving to reduce choking risk
About this recipe These are culinary cousins — both trace back to Indian indentured-labor cooking traditions, but diverged: Indian moong dal vada uses skinned split mung bean and curry leaves; Mauritian gâteau piment traditionally uses yellow split peas and thyme (often chives too, swapped here for cilantro to stay onion/garlic-free for Jain). Toor dal isn't offered as a third option — it's too soft and crumbly to hold together fried this way; it's better suited to dal fry or sambar. Texture check: batter should hold together without added water — too wet, add a spoon of chickpea flour; too dry, add a few drops of water at a time.
Appliance & storage Stovetop kadai works best for the adult batch either way. For a lighter version in the Ninja Crispi, brush patties with oil and air-fry at 350°F for 8–10 minutes, flipping halfway — add 2–3 minutes for the split-pea version, and check a bit early since the Crispi runs hot. Toddler patties stay stovetop pan-seared, not air-fried; their thin shape dries out fast in the Crispi. Leftover vadas keep 3 days refrigerated and re-crisp nicely in a dry pan, or the Crispi at 330°F for 3–4 minutes.
